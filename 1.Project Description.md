# Indigo Airlines Booking Data Analysis

---

# Overview

This project focuses on analyzing Indigo Airlines’ booking and operational data to understand passenger behavior, booking patterns, operational efficiency, and revenue distribution.

The analysis was performed using Excel by applying data cleaning, exploratory data analysis (EDA), pivot tables, correlation analysis, and dashboarding techniques to identify actionable business insights.

The project helps Indigo Airlines improve route profitability, optimize scheduling, enhance customer experience, and support data-driven business decisions.

---

# Problem Statement

Indigo Airlines operates in a highly competitive aviation industry and aims to:
- Optimize flight operations
- Improve customer satisfaction
- Increase booking conversion
- Maximize route profitability
- Understand passenger preferences

The objective of this project was to analyze airline booking data and identify operational trends, customer behavior patterns, and revenue opportunities that can improve business performance and operational efficiency.

# Dataset

The dataset contains Indigo Airlines booking and operational data including:

| Column Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Passenger Details | Number of passengers and booking behavior |
| Booking Information | Booking amount, booking completion |
| Customer Preferences | Extra baggage, meals, preferred seat |
| Flight Information | Route, flight duration, flight day, flight hour |
| Operational Data | Planned vs actual flight duration |
| Revenue Metrics | Seat class revenue and booking amount |
| Customer Segments | Trip type, booking origin, sales channel |

## Data Types Used

| Column | Data Type |
|---|---|
| Flight Duration Planned | Numerical Continuous |
| Booking Amount | Numerical Discrete |
| Num Passengers | Numerical Discrete |
| Flight Day | Categorical Ordinal |
| Seat Class | Categorical Ordinal |
| Sales Channel | Categorical Nominal |

The dataset includes both categorical and numerical variables used for customer behavior and operational analysis.

# Hypotheses Tested

## Accepted Hypotheses
- Higher seat classes generate higher booking amounts.
- Customers opting for additional services have higher booking amounts.
- Business class customers tend to book earlier than economy passengers.

## Rejected Hypotheses
- Weekend travel significantly increases economy bookings.
- Internet users always generate higher booking values than mobile users.
- Longer stays always lead to higher booking amounts.

Hypothesis testing was performed using pivot analysis, comparative analysis, and correlation techniques.

---

# Key Insights

## Customer Preference Insights
- Around 74.5% of passengers preferred extra baggage services.
- Additional services significantly increased booking value.
- Bookings were almost evenly distributed across all seat classes.

## Operational Insights
- Routes like KIX, CIN, and SIN showed the highest flight duration variation.
- Flight delays appeared independent of flight frequency.

## Booking Trend Insights
- Booking origins like Malta and Bhutan showed very high purchase lead time.
- Purchase lead time remained relatively stable across seat classes and stay durations.

## Revenue Insights
- Business class contributed approximately 41.9% of total revenue.
- Routes like PEN, PVG, KIX, and ICN generated the highest booking revenue.

## Passenger Load Insights
- Weekday flights had significantly higher passenger load compared to weekends.
- Morning flights consistently showed higher occupancy.

Key insights were generated through dashboard visualization and pivot-based analysis.

# Results & Conclusion

This project demonstrates how Excel can be effectively used for airline business analytics and operational decision-making.

The analysis revealed that:
- Business class and premium routes drive the majority of revenue.
- Ancillary services create strong upselling opportunities.
- Weekday and morning flights experience higher passenger demand.
- Internet channels dominate booking conversion.
- Operational inefficiencies are concentrated on specific routes rather than flight volume.

The project highlights the importance of customer behavior analysis, operational optimization, and route-level profitability monitoring in improving airline performance.
